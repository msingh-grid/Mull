Studio Paper

A manuscript on a well-lit desk: Mull marks up your words the way a careful editor does, in red pencil and blue-black ink, and every mark is legible before it lands.

Warm paper ground, a book voice for the human's words, a system voice for the machine's labels, and a mono voice for the diff. Color appears only where it carries meaning: deletion, insertion, warning, memory.

The HUD, three states, at real size

A non-activating floating panel, 480px wide, on its own paper ground with a true shadow. Shown over three wallpapers, from bright to dark, to prove the panel never borrows legibility from what is behind it.

One journal entry

The journal is the ledger of the desk: every dictation, edit, and command as a single scannable line, before/after on demand, undo always one press away.

Why this fits trustworthy instrument

An editor's markup is the original legible diff: for a century, red pencil and ink on paper have meant "here is exactly what changes, and nothing changes until you agree." Studio Paper borrows that contract literally, so the HUD reads as a careful professional at your desk rather than a magician behind a curtain. Warmth here is not decoration; it is the difference between an instrument you monitor and one you work beside all day.

Dark mode resolves as the same desk after sundown: paper inverts to a warm lamplit charcoal#211C13 with cream ink#ECE5D3, the pencil, ink, ochre, and green hues keep their exact roles at raised luminance, hairlines lighten, and shadows deepen; the journal and settings follow the system appearance while the HUD may stay paper-light as a deliberate "page in the dark."
