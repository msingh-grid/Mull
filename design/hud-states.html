Every state the panel can be in

Mull now does three different things to your Mac, and the panel has been reporting all three the same way. This board gives each of them a material — so before reading a single word you already know whether the key you are about to press changes your document, moves somebody else's window, or does nothing at all.

Three things Mull does, and the card says which

The panel grew one label at a time and ended up with ten of them in a single slot — four activities, three card kinds, two faults and an outcome, all in the same 11px tracked grey. Nothing in the form of the word told you which kind you were reading.

Underneath that vocabulary the product actually does three things, and they differ in exactly the way a user cares about: what it costs to press the key. So the ground under the card carries it, and the words are free to say something else.

Marks
Mull writes into your document. Dictation, an edit, a draft, a send. The card sits on fresh paper — --paper-bright — carries the one filled button in the app, and promises the undo in the same breath.
Acts
Mull presses things in another app. A navigation plan, before you press Run. Fresh paper too, because something will happen — but the promise is different, and it is the true one: nothing is written or sent.
Reads
Mull tells you something. An answer, or a finished plan. The card is pressed into the desk — --paper-recessed — and there is no filled button anywhere on it, because a filled button is this app's word for this commits.

One rule to learn, and it is visible from across the room: fresh paper means a key changes something. A pressed-in well means nothing will. The Apply button that turned up under a summary of somebody's own notes could not have survived this — an answer has no fresh paper to put it on.

While it works

The panel used to say THINKING and nothing else for as long as twenty seconds, which is indistinguishable from a hang — and was reported as one. Two fixes, both here: the label names the activity, and the line under it names the step and starts counting once there is genuinely something to wait for.

One proposal in this section: INSERTING becomes WRITING, and READING joins the set. Inserting is the implementation's word for it; the product's own metaphor is an editor with a pencil, and a pencil writes.

Pressed into the desk, and no filled button anywhere on them. A filled button is this app's word for this commits, and there is nothing here to commit. ⏎ and esc both close.

Two faults, and they are not the same fault. Ochre is something you can fix — secure input is on, a permission is missing, the model is unreachable and the local rules are deciding. What this costs to build

Every item is additive to the shipped design system — no new tokens, no new fonts, no new geometry. The two that matter most are the first two, and together they are the fix for the bug that started this.

  1. A card declares its family. will paints --paper-bright, wont paints --paper-recessed and refuses a filled button. Derived from the card kind, not set by each lane, so a new lane cannot forget. src/renderer/components/Cards.tsx · src/renderer/hud.css
  2. The promise moves under its button. One line under the action row instead of margin-left:auto at the far right. src/renderer/hud.css · .undo-promise → .promise
  3. The answer body becomes serif. One declaration; it currently reads as an instrument label rather than as prose. src/renderer/hud.css · .card-body.is-answer
  4. A finished plan settles into the well. The card swaps family when the walk ends, which is also when FOUND stops being needed. src/renderer/hud/view-model.ts · labelFor
  5. PLAN and READING join; INSERTING becomes WRITING; FOUND goes. src/renderer/hud/view-model.ts · labelFor
  6. Error takes --del, Applied takes --mem. Two selectors; the hue system already carries the meanings. src/renderer/hud.css · .state-label
  7. The plan card reports where a press landed, which the executor already returns — the card still prints the label the model chose. src/main/pipeline/navigate.ts · objectOf
